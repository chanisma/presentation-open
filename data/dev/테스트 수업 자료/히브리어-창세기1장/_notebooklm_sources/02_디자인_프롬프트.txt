The style must strictly follow the visual language of a bright, friendly gamified language-learning mobile app (Duolingo-like), rendered as a flat vector UI mockup. The background of every slide is pure snow white (#ffffff) with generous empty space — never tinted, never gradient, never textured, no photographic elements, no drop shadows of the blurry kind.

The single dominant brand color is owl green (#58cc02) and it must occupy roughly 30% of each slide through buttons, banners, progress bars, badges, connector lines and highlighted card borders. Supporting colors are used sparingly and always for a fixed meaning: deep green (#58a700) only as the hard bottom shadow under green elements, pale green (#dbf8c5) as a soft fill for correct or emphasized surfaces, streak orange (#ff9600) for direction arrows and attention, gem purple (#ce82ff) for grammar-note labels, eel blue (#1cb0f6) for hints, cardinal red (#ff4b4b) for wrong answers and rupture, bee yellow (#ffc800) for light, stars and achievement. Text is eel black (#3c3c3c) for primary and wolf grey (#777777) for secondary. Never introduce a color outside this list.

The signature detail that must appear on every slide is the chunky tactile shadow: every card, button, banner and lesson bubble has a 2px solid grey (#e5e5e5) border on all sides and a 4px thick solid border only along its bottom edge, so each element reads like a physical 3D button waiting to be pressed. Borders are always thick and visible — hairlines are forbidden. Corner radii are friendly and consistent: 16px on cards and tiles, 12px on buttons and inputs, and fully rounded pill shapes (9999px) on chips, badges and progress bars. Lesson bubbles are perfect circles with a 6px darker bottom rim.

Typography is big, rounded and confident. Headings use a heavy rounded sans-serif at weight 800 and are noticeably larger than in a typical presentation — the deck never whispers. Body text is a clean neutral sans-serif at weight 400 to 500 with generous line spacing. Korean text is set in a rounded, highly legible sans-serif. Foreign-script vocabulary (Hebrew, Greek) is always presented as very large, clean, correctly formed display lettering inside a white card, and is always accompanied directly underneath by its Korean pronunciation in smaller grey text and its Korean meaning in medium black text — the three lines are never separated.

Illustrations are simple flat vector line drawings with uniform 3px strokes, rounded caps, filled shapes and no outlines heavier than the card borders — small hand-drawn-feeling icons such as a bird, a nest, a hammer, a scale, a calendar strip, a conveyor belt, a cracked heart, a seedling. A single friendly mascot may appear: a round owl-like bird in owl green with two large white eyes, small orange beak and short wings. The mascot is STRICTLY secondary decoration — at most one per slide, never taller than 18% of the slide height, always standing in an empty margin, never overlapping text, cards or diagrams, never the main subject, and absent entirely from slides about rupture or judgment.

The overall impression is a cheerful, uncluttered lesson screen from a language app blown up to presentation size: one dominant focal element per slide, big rounded type, thick friendly borders, chunky green buttons, and lots of clean white space around everything. Captured straight-on, perfectly flat, no perspective, no frame, no page border, no device mockup bezel.
