The style must strictly follow a clean, minimal white-tone vector illustration aesthetic — like a well-designed SVG infographic. The background is a soft off-white paper tone (#FDFDFB) with generous whitespace and absolutely no texture, no gradient mesh, no photographic elements. All diagrams, flowcharts, code boxes, graphs, and arrows are drawn as crisp flat vector shapes with uniform thin strokes (2-3px), rounded line caps, and no drop shadows. Tables and grids are rendered as clean bracketed or hairline layouts with tabular, evenly spaced text.

The accent palette is limited to exactly four soft colors used sparingly against the white: dusty teal (#3E9C93) for the primary concept and headings, warm coral (#E8705A) for emphasis, warnings, and common-mistake callouts, soft mustard (#E4B04A) for highlights and key results, and slate navy (#33415C) for body text and code. Never use more than three accent colors on a single slide. Large areas of pure white must remain empty.

Figural elements stay minimal and geometric: when a person, computer, or object must appear, draw it as a simple flat pictogram in the same thin-stroke vector language — rounded body, dot eyes at most, no detailed faces, no cartoon characters, no mascots. Pictograms are secondary to the diagram, small, and placed beside it, never overlapping text or code. Speech bubbles are short and never obscure the content.

The overall impression is a quiet, airy, modern educational poster: precise vector geometry carrying the content, clean geometric typography with generous line spacing, left-aligned. Captured straight-on, perfectly flat, no perspective, no frame, no border.
